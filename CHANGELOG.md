# Changelog

## v0.5.0 — 2026-11-26

- **A learning record, acting on nothing.** The engine now measures every room's **warm-ups** (a setpoint rise, or heating coming on, to at least 1° above the reading) and **cool-downs** (a drop to at least 1.5° below it, or heating going off): lag, rate in degrees per hour, time to reach the setpoint, and for a cool-down the Newton constant `k` when the outside temperature is known. The definitions, thresholds and the 45-minute skip at the start of a cool-down come from measuring a real house first: a radiator valve's own sensor reads the air beside the radiator, several degrees above the room while heating, so the first stretch of a cool-down is the radiator, not the room. All of it lives in the pure core (`heating_core/learning.py`), with tests on synthetic rooms; replayed against ten days of a real house's recorded history it gave the same rates as a hand analysis of the same data.
- **Readings per source.** Each room is followed through `device` (the climate entity's own `current_temperature`) and, when the room has a `temp_sensor`, `sensor` as well, side by side. Each episode remembers the entity it was read from and a summary counts only the current one, so changing a room's sensor starts a fresh count without discarding the old episodes.
- **Nothing is guessed.** An episode open at a restart, or whose readings go missing for over ten minutes, is dropped. One with no measurable rate is not kept. A summary needs ten episodes before it gives a figure in words; until then it says "Not enough warm-ups yet (4 of 10)". The newest 60 of each kind are kept per room and source, in the runtime state document, never in the config or `rev`. A failure in the record is logged once and never stops an evaluation.
- **State and API.** Each room in `GET state` gains `learning`: per source, `heat` and `cool` summaries (`count`, `needed`, `enough`, `rate` median and quartiles, `lag_min`, `reach_share` or `k`, `first`, `last`, and plain-words `text`), and `open`, the sources with an episode in progress. A new websocket command `heating_engine/learning` and view `GET /api/heating_engine/learning` return every kept episode. Each closed episode is logged at info level (`Learning: ...`).
- **Config:** an optional top-level `outside_sensor` (a numeric `sensor`, or a `weather` entity's `temperature` attribute), validated as an entity id, read only by the learning record. `temp_sensor` is now documented. Both additive.
- `features` gains `learning`. `api_version` stays 1.

## v0.4.1 — 2026-11-24

- **Fix: a setpoint change that lands on the schedule's own target is no longer a manual change.** Seen live in a controlled room whose thermostat still runs its own schedule: two seconds after the engine's block edge the thermostat stepped to the very value the engine wanted, the engine filed it as `manual_setpoint`, and the room showed "Changed at the radiator" at the schedule's own temperature for the whole block. Now a change the engine did not make is compared with what the rules ask for (`matches_plan` in `heating_core/resolve.py`): the room's target now and over the next 90 seconds, in `heat` mode, with boosts and holds counted and the room's own manual change left out. A match is not a manual change, is not added to `unexpected_changes` or the health count, and is logged at info level ("matches the schedule"); the room keeps the schedule's own reason. The grace covers a device schedule that moves a few seconds before or after the engine's block edge, in either order.
- **A manual change ends early when the radiator is put back to the schedule.** If a manual change is on and the setpoint is then changed to exactly what the schedule asks for, the manual change is dropped rather than re-asserted over the hand that just undid it.
- **No write for a target the plan is about to switch to.** When the valve already shows the value the plan asks for within the same 90 seconds, the engine waits for the edge instead of writing the old target and the new one seconds apart.
- Covered by tests on the pure helper: now, within the grace, two minutes early, mode and tolerance, the room's own override versus another room's, holds and boosts, a rota edge.

## v0.4.0 — 2026-11-24

- **An activity log, with who asked.** Every request the engine accepts is logged whether or not it leads to a write: a boost, a cancelled boost, a hold, a cancelled hold, an exception set or cleared, a config save. `GET log` (websocket and REST) gains `requests`: oldest first, bounded at 200, each `{when, room, person, action, detail, source, user}`. `detail` is plain words ("Boost to 21° for 15 min", "Skip until 19:00", "Hold at Eco until turned back on", "Frost protection until turned back on", "Set to 18° until 19:00", "Back to the schedule", "Away on Wed 25 Nov", "Schedule or settings changed"). A cancel or clear that found nothing to do is not logged.
- **`source` on every request.** The six services, the REST views `hold` and `cancel-hold`, `POST config` and the websocket `set_config` take an optional `source` (1 to 32 characters of `a-z` and `_`; the heating page sends `heating_page`). The engine also notes `user`, the Home Assistant user's name from the call or request context, `null` when there is none. `source` is kept on the boost or hold it creates (`source` on `boosts` and `holds` in `state`, and on a room's `hold`) and copied to every write that boost or hold causes, including the restore write after it ends.
- **`cause` and `source` on writes.** Each `writes` entry says why: `schedule`, `boost`, `hold`, `manual` (re-asserting a change made at the radiator) or `restore` (putting an observing room's setpoint back).
- **The logs survive a restart.** `would_write`, `writes` and `requests` are now stored with the runtime state, as `unexpected_changes` already was, each bounded at 200. Seen live: empty `writes` after every restart.
- **`features` in `GET state`.** A list of what this engine can do beyond the first shape of `api_version` 1, now `["holds", "activity"]`, so a page can send `source` only to an engine that accepts it. Everything in this release is additive; `api_version` stays 1.

## v0.3.1 — 2026-11-24

- **Fix: a calendar whose entity is `unavailable` could not be read, so trips never updated.** The engine called `calendar.get_events`, which goes through Home Assistant's entity service layer; that layer silently drops an entity whose state is `unavailable` and then raises "Service call requested response data but did not match any entities", every five minutes, for as long as the entity stays that way, while `GET /api/calendars/<entity>` kept answering with the current events. Seen live: a trip shortened in the calendar that the engine did not notice. The engine now reads the calendar entity directly, the way that REST view does (`calendar_read.py`), so an unavailable calendar still answers from what it last fetched; the engine logs once when a calendar is unavailable and once when it comes back. A mis-cased id, an id that is not a calendar entity, or an unknown entity now each produce a health problem that says so in words. The read is covered by tests with stand-ins for the two Home Assistant names it uses.
- **Docs: `hvac_action` is `null` for Tado X over Matter.** Home Assistant's Matter integration only fills `hvac_action` from the optional `ThermostatRunningState` attribute, which Tado X does not expose. The README says so, and that a dashboard should read `null` as "not reported", not "idle".

## v0.3.0 — 2026-11-24

- **Holds set from the dashboard, as resolution rule 2.** A new runtime object alongside boosts: `skip` (the room's Eco until the next scheduled change), `eco` (Eco until cancelled), `frost` (the room's new `temps.frost`, default 5°, until cancelled) and `temp` (a half-degree temperature until the next scheduled change). `until` may also be `indefinite` or an ISO datetime. The next scheduled change is the same moment a change at the radiator runs to: a block edge, an arrival or departure, a holiday edge. A hold sits below a boost (which wins while it runs, then the hold resumes) and above a change made at the radiator. A hand on the radiator replaces a dashboard hold and the reason text says so; setting a hold replaces an earlier hand on the radiator. Holds persist, survive a restart and end on their own at `until`. Like a boost, a hold writes to an observing room's valve and the earlier setpoint is put back after; a boost and a hold on the same observing room share one restore point. Reason text: "Skipping until 19:00", "Holding at Eco until you turn it back on", "Frost protection until you turn it back on", "Set to 18° until 19:00".
- **The valve-read manual hold moves from rule 2 to rule 3.** Its `reason_code` (`manual`) and text are unchanged; only the `rule` number moves, to keep the number ordered by priority. Rules 6 and 8 stay reserved; the open-window rule will take a number when it is built.
- **Services** `heating_engine.hold` (`room`, `kind`, `temp`, `until`) and `heating_engine.cancel_hold` (`room`). **REST** `POST /api/heating_engine/hold` and `POST /api/heating_engine/cancel-hold` call the same code and return the room's state entry and its hold.
- **State:** each room in `GET state` gains `hvac_action` (the climate entity's own `hvac_action`, verbatim, `null` when absent) and `hold` (`null` or `{kind, target, until, set_at}`); a top-level `holds` list mirrors `boosts`. The room target sensor gains `hvac_action`, `hold_kind` and `hold_until`; the health sensor gains `holds_active`. `api_version` stays 1: every change to the state and config documents is additive (`temps.frost` defaults when missing). The only existing field whose value changes is `rule` for a manual hold, noted above.
- Runtime state (boosts, holds, overrides) is serialised in `heating_core/persist.py`, with no Home Assistant imports, so the round trip is tested.

## v0.2.2 — 2026-11-24

- **The engine no longer mistakes its own write for someone else's.** `_write` recorded the expectation only after the blocking climate call returned, but a Matter device reports the new setpoint while that call is still awaiting, so the engine saw its own echo first, counted it as an unexpected change and, for a setpoint echo, installed a manual override at the value it had just written. Seen live: a boost whose end never restored the schedule because the boost's own echo had become a "manual hold". The expectation is now recorded before the call. A guard test keeps that order.

## v0.2.1 — 2026-11-23

- **The last successful calendar read is kept across restarts.** Seen live after a Home Assistant restart: the engine evaluated before the calendar integration had loaded, `calendar.get_events` was "not found", and with no events every calendar person counted as home for five minutes. Now the last known events are stored with the runtime state and used until a read succeeds, the integration loads after `calendar`, and while nothing is known a failed read is retried every minute rather than every five. The problem text says which it is: "using the last known trips" or "nobody is treated as away".

## v0.2.0 — 2026-11-23

- **A boost writes to the valve even while the room is observing.** It is an explicit, time-bounded request. For an observing room the engine remembers the setpoint the valve held before the boost and puts it back when the boost ends or is cancelled, instead of its own schedule target. A room in control simply resumes its schedule.
- **REST views** under `/api/heating_engine/{state,config,plan,log}` with the same contract as the websocket commands, for clients without a websocket (the heating page's server). `GET state` returns rooms, people, boosts, exceptions and health in one call.
- `set_config` no longer requires an admin user, over websocket or REST: any authenticated Home Assistant user may save schedules. The household decides who holds a token.
- Health sensor gains `boosts_active`.
- CI: pytest, byte-compile and manifest checks run on every push and tag.
- Guard test: `heating_core` must import nothing outside the standard library, and the repo must contain no IP addresses or token shapes.
